Marginalia

Notes left in the margins of borrowed books.

A small, quiet corner of the internet where we collect sentences worth keeping — and write down what we actually think about them.

What You'll Find Here

This site is a class project built by two students who like old libraries, long sentences, and dim reading lamps more than they probably should. It works like a shared commonplace book — a place to gather lines from what we're reading and keep a short, honest reaction underneath each one.

  • Short excerpts pulled from novels, essays, and philosophy we're reading
  • Our own reflections written underneath each excerpt
  • A running reading log that we update as we finish (or abandon) books
  • A form where visitors can send in a line worth sharing

Featured Excerpt

“It is a truth universally acknowledged.”

— Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice

One of the driest opening lines in literature, and still one of the funniest. We picked it as our first entry because half of this whole project is just us underlining sentences and writing "lol, true" next to them in nicer words.
